Give Through a Tax Credit

Turn your tax liability into scholarships

Louisiana and federal law let you direct tax dollars into K–12 scholarships. Gifts go to an approved Scholarship Granting Organization, which awards scholarships that families may use at Heritage Classical Academy.

Beginning January 1, 2027

Federal Education Freedom Tax Credit

Donors receive a dollar-for-dollar federal tax credit of up to $1,700 per year (up to $3,400 for married couples filing jointly) for gifts to an approved Scholarship Granting Organization. Heritage partners with ACE Scholarships. Unused credit carries forward up to five years, and Louisiana has opted in to the program.

You can schedule a single or recurring gift now through Heritage’s ACE portal. Payments activate in 2027.

Louisiana Tuition Donation Credit

Donate to a Louisiana School Tuition Organization such as ACE Scholarships or Arete Scholars and receive a 95% Louisiana state income tax credit. The remaining portion may qualify for a federal charitable deduction. Unused credit carries forward up to three years.

Common Questions

Education Freedom Tax Credit FAQs

What opportunities will this create for our school?
The Education Freedom Tax Credit (EFTC) will strengthen educational opportunities for our community, bolster our school’s sustainability, and generate a new stream of revenue that helps reduce the financial burden of educational expenses for our families. If just 100 people gave the full $1,700, it would generate $170,000 in student support.
What is a qualified contribution, and how much can I give?
Individuals receive a 100% dollar-for-dollar federal tax credit of up to $1,700 (up to $3,400 for married couples filing jointly) when they contribute to a certified Scholarship Granting Organization (SGO). Our school’s SGO is ACE Scholarships. Contributors cannot claim both the credit and a charitable deduction for the same dollars.
What educational expenses are covered?
Scholarship funds may be used for qualifying expenses connected with an eligible student’s enrollment or attendance at a school, including tuition, books and supplies, tutoring, education technology, and other qualifying expenses. Additional guidance from the U.S. Treasury on eligible expenses is expected.
Can parents give and direct the scholarship to their own children?
No. To ensure fairness, transparency, and compliance with federal law, parents cannot direct EFTC contributions to benefit their own children; the program prohibits any form of self-dealing. Dollars given through our school’s ACE portal will be used to create scholarships for Heritage students. Donations go first to our approved SGO (ACE Scholarships), which verifies student eligibility and awards scholarships.
How does the EFTC affect religious liberty and private school autonomy?
The EFTC incentivizes private investment in student scholarships. Unlike traditional voucher programs, it does not use public funds appropriated by the state or federal government. This approach expands educational freedom for students and protects against government infringement of religious liberty, consistent with the U.S. Supreme Court’s ruling in Espinoza v. Montana Department of Revenue (2020).
How will the federal government pay for these credits?
The EFTC is funded entirely through voluntary private contributions, not new government spending. It expands educational opportunity without taking a single dollar from public schools.
Who is ACE Scholarships?
ACE Scholarships is Heritage’s Scholarship Granting Organization partner. For more than 25 years, ACE has worked to expand educational opportunities, helping parents choose the learning environment that best meets their child’s unique needs. Learn more about ACE and the EFTC or read the U.S. Department of Education fact sheet.

Scholarships are awarded by the granting organization to eligible students, and gifts may not be directed to a specific student. This information is provided for general guidance only — please consult your tax preparer about your situation.
